# Calibrate a stable rank correction on training data

The distinct cross-encoder supplies a positive signal on the first 450 queries and a flat effect at a fixed weight on the [242-query comparison](FIFTH_RANKER_20261127.md#locked-242-query-transfer). Test whether the strength and shape of its score are suitable for reordering the tail. The original 192-token input, checkpoint, excerpts, and scoring budget are fixed. Rank one and all ten document IDs are fixed for every configuration.

Fit the choice of score transformation and weight on the already observed 692 training topics. Test standardized raw logits, sigmoid probabilities, and within-list descending rank discounts, each at weight 0.125, 0.25, 0.375, and 0.5. Include the maintained rank and the prior raw 0.25 rule as controls. Use domain-macro nDCG@10 as the selection objective. A policy must improve relative to the maintained rank after omitting any domain and any of the four panels. Also report leave-panel-out predictions from choosing settings on the other panels. Advance a single pooled setting only when that cross-fitted effect is positive and the pooled candidate improves over the old fixed weight. Confirm it on the separate 191-question panel, with exactly that setting and the same two controls.

The original raw-logit 0.25 setting remains the strongest at **0.31215** versus **0.31004** for the maintained rank. The settings chosen on other panels score 0.31051. Their gain is +0.00047 [−0.00199, +0.00294], and removing one domain can reverse it. No fitted setting advances. ## Additional replication of the original fixed rule

The sole transferred rule has one positive discovery result, one effectively flat comparison, and no stronger alternative after the score-form audit. Evaluate this unchanged rule on the 191 questions absent from both of those panels. Compare only against their maintained order. In this follow-up the score uses the same 192-token prefix, model and excerpt procedure. Score only the existing ten documents, since both z-normalizations use those same ten. Report this as an additional replication after the flat 242 result. It keeps all 242 outcomes in the overall transfer estimate. Consider a single official replay only if both the 191 and the pooled 433 have positive domain macros and remain positive when omitting any one domain. The 191 were used in other project experiments.

The 191-question replay scores **0.28162 versus 0.28212**, a change of −0.00050 [−0.00413, +0.00308]. The pooled 433 score is 0.27044 versus 0.27046, a change of −0.00002 [−0.00210, +0.00198]. These scores leave the official reference in place.
