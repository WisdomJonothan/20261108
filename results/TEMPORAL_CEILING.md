# Ceiling analysis: what could a perfect year-consistency reranker add?

Generated by `scripts/temporal_ceiling.py`; per-domain JSONs are
`results/temporal_ceiling_<domain>_dev.json`.

For every anchored dev query we take the frozen dense top-100 and reorder it
using **only** the query's year interval and the document's extracted year —
no qrels, no gold information — then recompute nDCG@10.

Two oracles:

* `perfect` — time-consistent documents first, then inconsistent, then unknown.
* `demote` — keep the original order, only push year-inconsistent documents to
  the end (the realistic version, since most documents have no extractable year).

If a temporal reranker is ever going to help, these numbers are its optimistic
upper bound.

| domain | dense | perfect | demote | mismatch rate* | anchored q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| politics | 0.3074 | 0.2507 | 0.2677 | 63.0% | 26 |
| economics | 0.1891 | **0.2164** | **0.2013** | 58.8% | 9 |
| genealogy | 0.2110 | 0.1740 | 0.1838 | 78.1% | 17 |
| hsm | 0.2926 | 0.2252 | 0.2705 | 78.8% | 10 |
| history | 0.2772 | 0.2204 | 0.2692 | 71.6% | 98 |
| bitcoin | 0.2171 | 0.1651 | 0.1967 | 69.6% | 6 |
| law | 0.3013 | 0.2895 | 0.2911 | 100% | 2 |
| workplace | 0.3795 | 0.3341 | 0.3805 | 53.3% | 1 |

\* share of top-100 candidates **with an extractable year** whose year lies
outside the query interval (±1). Candidates without a year are excluded from
this denominator.

## The finding

In 6 of 8 domains even the *oracle* time-consistency ordering **lowers**
nDCG@10, and the realistic "demote inconsistent documents" variant lowers it in
5 of 8 (economics is the only clear winner; workplace is flat).

The reason is visible in the mismatch column: among candidates whose year can be
extracted, 53–100% carry a year outside the query's interval — and many of those
are **gold**. In this benchmark a document that mentions another period is
frequently still the right answer (background, comparison, retrospective), so
"time mismatch" is not a usable negative signal.

This is consistent with, and explains, every earlier negative result:

* the 37–43 mined hard negatives across five domains (≈0.4 per anchored query),
* the year-feature ablation (sign flips between bitcoin and economics),
* the semantic temporal-consistency probe (ΔAUC ≈ 0),
* the naive TACR rerank collapsing the ranking.

The honest claim for the paper: **content-time consistency, as extractable from
document text, is not a reliable relevance signal in RETECO Track 1a.** The
limitation is the benchmark's construction (gold documents are answers to
questions, not period-restricted documents), not only our extractor.
