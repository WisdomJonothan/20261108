# Ceiling analysis: what could a perfect year-consistency reranker add?

Generated by `scripts/temporal_ceiling.py` on the **mxbai** dense top-100 (the
deployed first stage). Per-domain JSONs are
`results/temporal_ceiling_mxbai_<domain>_dev.json`; each summary records the
`run` path and `k` it was computed from.

For every anchored dev query we take the frozen dense top-100 and reorder it
using **only** the query's year interval and the document's extracted year —
no qrels, no gold information — then recompute nDCG@10.

Two oracles:

* `perfect` — time-consistent documents first, then inconsistent, then unknown.
* `demote` — keep the original order, only push year-inconsistent documents to
  the end (the realistic version, since most documents have no extractable year).

If a temporal reranker is ever going to help, these numbers are its optimistic
upper bound.

| domain | dense | perfect | demote | mismatch rate* | anchored q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| politics | 0.3599 | 0.2598 | 0.3123 | 57.6% | 26 |
| economics | 0.1890 | **0.1930** | **0.1982** | 57.4% | 9 |
| genealogy | 0.3298 | 0.2541 | 0.3035 | 77.5% | 17 |
| hsm | 0.3125 | 0.2628 | 0.3024 | 81.8% | 10 |
| history | 0.3051 | 0.2477 | 0.2970 | 68.7% | 98 |
| bitcoin | 0.1820 | 0.1556 | 0.1631 | 64.8% | 6 |
| law | 0.3689 | 0.3337 | 0.3536 | 100% | 2 |
| workplace | 0.3362 | 0.2916 | 0.3371 | 73.7% | 1 |
| **macro (8 anchored domains)** | **0.2980** | **0.2498** | **0.2834** | | **169** |

\* share of top-100 candidates **with an extractable year** whose year lies
outside the query interval (±1). Candidates without a year are excluded from
this denominator.

## The finding

The *oracle* time-consistency ordering lowers nDCG@10 in **7 of 8** domains, and
the realistic "demote inconsistent documents" variant lowers it in **6 of 8**
(economics is the only clear winner; workplace is flat, +0.0009).

The reason is visible in the mismatch column: among candidates whose year can be
extracted, 57–100% carry a year outside the query's interval — and many of those
are **gold**. In this benchmark a document that mentions another period is
frequently still the right answer (background, comparison, retrospective), so
"time mismatch" is not a usable negative signal.

This is consistent with, and explains, every earlier negative result:

* the 37–43 mined hard negatives across five domains (≈0.4 per anchored query),
* the year-feature ablation (sign flips between bitcoin and economics),
* the semantic temporal-consistency probe (ΔAUC ≈ 0),
* the naive TACR rerank collapsing the ranking.

The honest claim for the paper: **content-time consistency, as extractable from
document text, is not a reliable relevance signal in RETECO Track 1a.** The
limitation is the benchmark's construction (gold documents are answers to
questions, not period-restricted documents), not only our extractor.

## Provenance / correction (2026-11-09)

The first version of this note used an older dense candidate run (not the mxbai
stage that ships) and miscounted its own table ("6 of 8" / "5 of 8" where the
table showed 7 and 6). Both are fixed above; the conclusion is unchanged and now
holds on the deployed candidate set. The ceiling is an *upper bound* over
candidate orderings only — it says nothing about recall, and it is measured on
the 169 anchored queries of 8 domains, not on all 519.

## What the mismatched gold documents actually are (2026-11-09)

The `mismatch rate` column is a *definition*, not a fact, so we coded a sample
by hand (`results/MISMATCH_ANNOTATION.md`, sheet and labels in
`results/mismatch/`). 53 blinded items, four independent coders, two of them
double-coding the same 20 items (Cohen's κ = 0.84).

Two definitions are in play:

* **def1** — the query interval is built from its *largest* year (what
  `temporal_ceiling.py` and `hardneg_mine.py` use); it flags **120 / 246**
  year-bearing dev gold documents (48.8%).
* **def2** — the interval spans `[min year − 1, max year + 1]` when the query
  names several years; it flags **73 / 246** (29.7%). 47 documents change side.

18 of the 53 sampled items are def1 artefacts (the query names the document's
own year, e.g. "What did X do in 2017?" against a 2017 document). On the
remaining **35 strict mismatches**:

| label | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| retrospective (mentions an older period as background) | 13 | 37.1% |
| incidental_year (year mention is not the topic) | 11 | 31.4% |
| continuing (mechanism still current, one dated event) | 8 | 22.9% |
| period_shift (answers a different period) | 2 | 5.7% |
| wrong_time (genuinely the wrong period) | 1 | 2.9% |

So **34 of 35 year-mismatched gold documents are still correct answers**, and
the ceiling's mismatch column over-states genuine period mismatch by roughly
19 percentage points (48.8% vs 29.7%). This is the empirical mechanism behind
the table above: an oracle that demotes "inconsistent" documents demotes gold.
The only temporal lever that could still work needs *event-time* extraction
from prose (what the document asserts happened when), not publication-year
matching — which is exactly the signal the benchmark does not expose.
