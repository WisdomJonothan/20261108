# Temporal observability: where can a temporal module even be measured?

Generated by `scripts/temporal_coverage.py` → `results/temporal_coverage_dev.json`.

The question this answers: our earlier temporal experiments were run mostly on
Bitcoin/Economics, but a temporal reranker can only help if (a) the query states
a time constraint and (b) the documents carry the time they talk about. This
table measures both, per domain, on the dev split.

## What this changes

Bitcoin — the domain most of our temporal experiments used — is one of the
*worst* places to look for a temporal effect: only 19% of its gold documents
carry a year at all, and only 20% of its dev queries state one. A module that
judges "topic relevant but time wrong" has almost nothing to judge there.

The domains where the experiment is actually identifiable are politics
(84.7% gold coverage), economics (64.2%), genealogy (56.4%), hsm (52.2%) and
history (37.5%, but 240 queries, so the largest absolute sample).

Consequence for the paper: the earlier negative results (year features, temporal
semantic consistency, query decomposition) should be re-read as "no gain on
domains where temporal consistency is largely unobservable", not as "temporal
modelling does not work in RETECO". The new experiments must be pre-registered
on the high-coverage domains, with bitcoin reported as a low-coverage control.
